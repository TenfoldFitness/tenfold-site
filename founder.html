4Part 4 · How I decide

I name the problem, then set the limits.

Three projects for outside teams. For each one I wrote down the problem, scoped the solution, and made one call about what to leave out or not claim.

KeHE

Forecasting food trends, with a person in the loop

Problem. Buyers need an early read on which food trends are real.

Scope. A buyer dashboard that scores each trend out of 100 and leaves every decision to a person.

Tools. Python, FastAPI, LlamaIndex, Nixtla

The judgment call

The detector flags and a person decides. A repository advertising cultural-momentum scoring was an unmodified fork of a 671B language model with no such feature, so I cut it. The tool ships with real products and no fabricated figures.

JPMorgan Chase

One shared record, so nobody falls out of a workshop

Problem. No single record of who is in a workshop and where they stand.

Scope. One row per person, matched on email then phone, with one status every facilitator can read.

Tools. Airtable, Automations, QR check-in

The judgment call

An ambiguous match is flagged, never guessed. A walk-in with no contact details counts toward headcount and is excluded from every outbound send.

Checkr

Telling government buyers what Checkr can actually do

Problem. Agencies need to know what Checkr can do before they spend review time.

Scope. A pre-suitability portal that runs before review. The agency keeps every decision.

Tools. Framer, FCRA review pass, OPM hiring framework

The judgment call

The brief wanted “compliant hiring.” Checkr supports compliance and does not guarantee it, so the page says so.
