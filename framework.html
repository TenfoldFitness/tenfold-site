The Tenfold Framework

Three pillars, one method

A clinically grounded approach that treats physical training and mental acuity as one and the same project, not two separate goals.

Where it started

The Tenfold Framework began with the aging-in-place model: a movement philosophy built around helping people maintain strength, mobility, and independence in their own lives, on their own terms, for as long as possible. The roles and routines that give life meaning often change with age, but they don't have to lose their meaning. The goal was always to help people keep showing up for their own lives.

Over time, that same philosophy expanded to meet people wherever they are, not only later in life. The physical side of the framework kept its aging-in-place foundation: functional movement, balance, and strength that hold up over decades. The psychological side was built alongside it, grounded in self-determination theory, the model built around the three needs proven to drive lasting behavior change.

The result is one method, built for the long game: strength training and mental acuity coaching, designed together instead of separately. This isn't a personal trainer offering casual mindset tips on the side. Both sides of the method are delivered by someone formally trained and credentialed in each discipline.

Why body and mind are trained together

Physical programming and mental acuity coaching aren't offered side by side as two separate services. They're built as one system, and there's a reason for that: research consistently shows that autonomy, competence, and relatedness aren't independent boxes to check. They work together, or not much works at all. Progress in one area (say, feeling more capable in the gym) tends to spill over into the others (feeling more in control of your choices, more connected to the people supporting you) and vice versa.

This also shows up in how motivation itself behaves. Change driven by pressure, guilt, or an outside reward tends to fade the moment that pressure or reward disappears. Change that's driven by something genuinely your own, a goal you actually value, tends to stick. So instead of relying on willpower or external accountability alone, every program is built to help that motivation become truly yours: internalized, self-directed, and far more likely to last.

The three pillars

Every program, every session, and every conversation is built around these three psychological needs.

Pillar 01

Autonomy

You're in the driver's seat. Programs are built around your life, your schedule, and your goals, not a one-size-fits-all plan handed down to you. This matters more than it might seem: people who feel a plan genuinely belongs to them tend to stick with it far longer than people just following orders or chasing an external reward. The goal isn't compliance, it's ownership.

Pillar 02

Competence

Progress you can actually feel. Every session is calibrated so you're challenged without being overwhelmed, so confidence builds step by step, right alongside physical strength. Feeling capable isn't just a nice side effect, it's one of the strongest predictors of whether someone keeps going or gives up.

Pillar 03

Relatedness

A real relationship, not just a client number. Feeling genuinely supported by someone who's in it with you, not just scheduling and billing you, is consistently linked to better follow-through and better outcomes, physically and emotionally both.
